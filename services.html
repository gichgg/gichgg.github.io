Engagements

Three ways in.

Each has a written scope agreed before any work — or any number — is committed.

Diagnostic · 1–2 weeks

Automation audit

I map where your people team's time actually goes and identify which processes are worth automating — with estimated hours recovered against build cost for each.

  • Process walkthrough with the people doing the work
  • Ranked shortlist of automation candidates
  • Effort, cost and hours-saved estimate for each

Ends with a recommendation, including the option to build nothing.

Build · 4–8 weeks

Process automation build

One named process, automated end to end. Report generation, data pipelines, candidate summarisation, structured screening — whatever the audit says earns its cost.

  • Written scope agreed before any work begins
  • Built, tested and handed over with documentation
  • Your team trained to run it without me

Fixed fee against the cost of the current process. Change requests priced separately.

Build · 3–6 weeks

People analytics reporting

A reporting layer your leadership team actually uses — headcount, attrition, pipeline, time-to-hire — refreshing itself from the systems where the data already lives.

  • Metric definitions agreed up front, so numbers stop being argued about
  • Automated refresh, no monthly rebuild
  • Built around the decisions being made, not every metric available

Power BI, Excel or a self-hosted dashboard, depending on what you already run.

Process

What happens, in order.

Step one

Scoping call

Thirty minutes on the process itself — who touches it, how often, how long it takes. No pricing discussed in this conversation.

Step two

Written scope and fee

A short document: what gets built, what it will and won't do, what I need from you, the date, and the fixed price. Nothing starts until it's signed.

Step three

Build and handover

Delivered async with a weekly written update. Handover includes documentation and a session with whoever will run it. New requirements become a new scope, not scope creep.

Fit

When not to hire me.

Cheaper for both of us to find this out now.

The process isn't stable yet

Automating a workflow nobody agrees on just makes the disagreement faster. Fix the process first — I'll tell you if that's where you are.

You need someone in the room daily

I deliver async, in defined blocks. If you need embedded, business-hours presence, you need a hire or a firm — not me.

The decision needs a human

Some judgement shouldn't be automated, particularly around assessment and selection. I'd rather lose the work than build something that quietly degrades a decision.

Which process is costing you the most time?

Tell me about it and I'll tell you whether it's worth automating.
